I am currently working as a post-doc researcher at the LIPh with Dr. D. M. Busiello. Our project focuses on the dynamics of out-of-equilibrium enzymatic media. I am interested in the study of complex systems, with particular emphasis on problems of biophysical interest.

I have a background in theoretical physics, which I studied at the University of Florence. I obtained my bachelor’s degree with a thesis about the Everett formulation of quantum mechanics with Prof. A.L. Cotrone. Then I got interested in the physics of complex systems. I obtained my master’s degree under the supervision of Prof. D. Fanelli, studying the emergence of stochastic resonance in a system featuring bistability induced by multiplicative noise.

I attained my PhD in physics in the very interdisciplinary context of a cotutelle agreement between the Universities of Orléans (France) and Florence, supervised by Prof. F.Piazza and Prof. J. Hamacek. In Orléans, I worked at the Centre de Biophysique Moléculaire (CNRS), in direct contact with experimental colleagues, while in Florence I was part of the Centro interdipartimentale per lo Studio di Dinamiche Complesse.  My project focused on the theoretical and experimental study of macromolecular crowding effects on enzymatic activity in the extracellular matrix. I worked on topics ranging from the physical properties of polymer solutions, with a special focus on branched polymers, to the analysis and interpretation of spectrofluorometric experiments.
